# Meta Ads: what to check per lens

Facts Meta documents publicly. Check the current Business Help Center when something looks off; Meta renames objectives and defaults often.

## X against Y
- The **performance goal and conversion event are set per ad set.** Read each one; campaigns often mix them.
- Meta needs enough of the chosen event to learn (it states about 50 per ad set per week to leave the learning phase). An event too rare at the bottom of the funnel can lose to the step above it.
- Lead campaigns on **instant forms** get cheap leads that are often lower quality than website leads. Conversion leads optimization and a CRM connection send quality back.
- Sales campaigns without purchase values cannot optimize for value.

## Setup
- Advantage+ placements and audience expand delivery past the settings someone typed. Know which are on before judging targeting.
- The attribution setting (for example 7-day click and 1-day view) is set per ad set. Comparing ad sets or periods with different settings compares different rulers.
- Special ad categories (housing, employment, credit, politics) restrict targeting by law; their results do not compare with normal ad sets.

## Measurement
- Pixel and Conversions API together must share an event id to deduplicate; without it, results double.
- Event Match Quality shows how well events match people; low quality weakens optimization.
- View-through conversions are part of the default attribution; compare against the business's own sales before crediting them.

## Structure and spend
- **Significant edits reset learning** (creative, targeting, optimization event, large budget changes). Many small edits keep an ad set from ever learning.
- Too many ad sets on one budget split the data; "Learning limited" is the signal.
- Breakdowns worth a look: age and gender, placement, platform and device, region, hour (in the ad account's time zone). Breakdown rows can be modeled and may not add up exactly to the total.

## Creative
- Meta concentrates spend on a few ads; that is normal. The question is whether the ads getting the money are the ones bringing Y.
- Fatigue shows as frequency rising while click rate and conversion rate fall.
- Vertical 9:16 fits Stories and Reels; a square or horizontal ad there wastes the screen.

## Audience
- Overlapping ad sets compete in the same auctions and raise each other's costs.
- Exclude recent purchasers from prospecting unless repeat purchase is the goal.
- Retargeting windows should match the sales cycle, not a default.

## Budget and pacing
- Campaign budget (Advantage+ campaign budget) moves money between ad sets toward the cheapest results by the chosen event, which may not be the ones closest to Y.
- Lifetime budgets can spend unevenly across days; daily budgets can spend up to 75% more on a good day, balanced over the week.
