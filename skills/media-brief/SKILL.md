# Media brief

The question is not "how many conversions does the account get" but "what does this business need from paid media, and how close is the signal the platforms optimize for to that".

Ad platforms buy exactly the event they are told to buy. If that event is a page view, a form or an add-to-cart while the business lives on closed sales, the account can look efficient and still miss. Every later step (plan, audit, critique) is measured against the brief, so it comes first.

## What to find out, and why

- **How money comes in.** One-off online sale, a lead a salesperson closes, a subscription, a store visit, an app user who pays later. This decides whether a platform conversion is the result or a step before it.
- **The real outcome (Y).** The thing that pays: revenue, qualified leads, closed sales, retained users. It is rarely the "conversions" column.
- **What one Y is worth.** Target cost per result or return on spend, margin, lead-to-sale rate, average order. Without it nothing can be called waste or a win.
- **The signal (X).** Which event each platform receives, how many steps it sits from Y (a form is often two steps from a sale; a purchase with its value is one), and how far to trust it: is it the primary goal, deduplicated, imported from the CRM, inside an attribution window that fits the sales cycle.
- **Stage.** Is the business creating demand or capturing demand that already exists. It changes which campaigns make sense.
- **Limits.** Markets, brand rules, things already tried and dropped, who approves changes.

## Where the answers come from

Context first, questions second. If anything connected holds the client's context (a client profile, a CRM, a brief, meeting notes, email), read it before asking. Ask only what is missing and would change a recommendation: three questions at most, one at a time.

Do not infer Y from the account's conversion column. That column is X, the thing being checked. When a line comes from data rather than from a person, mark it as inferred.

In markets with high inflation, store every money target with its date and currency. A cost-per-lead target set six months ago is not today's target.

## Done well

Five lines, each with where it came from (said by whom, seen in data, or assumed):

```
Business:      what it sells, to whom, how money comes in
Real outcome:  Y, and what one is worth (date, currency)
Signal:        X sent to each platform; steps from Y; how far to trust it
Stage:         creating or capturing demand
Unknown:       what is still missing and who can answer it
```

If X and Y differ, say it in the brief. The audit starts there.
